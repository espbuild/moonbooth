# Competitor research

Use this workflow when the user asks for a comparison of companies or
products in one market.

## Steps

1. **Set the scope.** Write down the market, the companies in scope,
   the segments that matter to the user, and the date of the research.
   If the user did not name segments, ask before going further.

2. **Gather per-company notes.** For each company, record: what it
   sells, who it sells to, its stated positioning (the words the
   company uses about itself), pricing facts, and the features the
   user cares about. Keep the source for every fact next to the fact:
   a page, a document, or a person who said it, plus the date seen.

3. **Separate claims from evidence.** A claim is what a company says
   about itself or a competitor. Evidence is a source a reader can
   check. In your notes, mark each item as `claim` or `evidence`.
   Never present a claim as a finding. When two sources disagree,
   record both and say they disagree.

4. **Compare.** Build two tables:
   - Feature comparison: one row per feature, one column per company.
     A cell holds `yes`, `no`, or `unknown`, plus a short note when
     the answer needs a condition.
   - Segment comparison: one row per segment, one column per company,
     stating whether the company sells to that segment and any proof
     of it (named customers, a pricing tier, a product page).
   Use `unknown` when you have no source. Do not guess to fill a
   cell.

5. **Write positioning notes.** For each company, write two or three
   sentences: the buyer it aims at, the problem it leads with, and
   how it differs from the others in the set, based on the evidence
   gathered. Mark any sentence that rests on a claim rather than
   evidence.

6. **Write the report.** Use the structure in
   `references/example-output.md`: scope, summary, feature table,
   segment table, positioning notes, open questions. Keep the summary
   to what the tables support.

7. **State missing information.** End the report with a section that
   lists every `unknown` cell, every claim you could not check, and
   every source that was out of date. Name what would settle each
   item (a pricing page, a customer reference, a trial account).

## Rules

- Date every fact. Pricing and features change; an undated fact is a
  defect in the report.
- Prefer primary sources (the company's own pages, filings, product
  documentation) over roundups and review sites.
- If the user gives you private material about a company, keep it out
  of any shared output unless the user says to include it.
